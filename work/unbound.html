UNBOUND

Designing a contemporary redesign of the QAGOMA website for art discovery and cultural engagement.

UNBOUND is an academic web design project where I redesigned the QAGOMA website as a more expressive and accessible digital experience for discovering exhibitions, events, gallery content, and cultural stories.

Overview

UNBOUND is a conceptual redesign of the QAGOMA (Queensland Art Gallery | Gallery of Modern Art) website, built as an academic project for DECO7140 Introduction to Web Design. The idea was to reimagine how a major cultural institution shows up online, making the experience feel more expressive, accessible, and genuinely engaging for contemporary audiences. Instead of a static information hub, UNBOUND treats the website as an immersive digital experience that reflects the energy and visual richness of the art itself.

Design Challenge

How do you design a cultural platform that balances visual immersion with functional clarity, making contemporary art feel discoverable without sacrificing the practical needs of browsing exhibitions, finding events, reading artist stories, and booking tickets?

Tools & Skills

I used Figma for interface planning and layout, Adobe Photoshop and Illustrator for visual assets, image treatment, branding, and graphic direction, and HTML, CSS, and JavaScript for the front-end prototype. The project drew on UX/UI design, editorial identity development, responsive web development, information architecture, accessibility (semantic HTML, ARIA labels, alt text), and modular CSS architecture. It was a constant balancing act between design expression and technical execution.

Research Scope

I studied how gallery, museum, and cultural event websites organise visual content and guide users toward meaningful action. I paid close attention to how exhibitions, events, artist profiles, newsletters, and editorial content were presented across multiple institutions, and where users seemed to lose context or get stuck trying to move between sections. That research shaped an information architecture built around five core areas: Home, Gallery, Events, Blog, and About Us.

Methodology

The project ran across 12 weeks, moving through research, information architecture, early prototyping, visual language development, and front-end build. I mapped key user journeys early: discovering a featured exhibition, browsing gallery content, checking upcoming events, reading cultural stories, and booking tickets. The visual direction centres on a bold black-and-white interface where the artwork and performance imagery bring all the colour and emotion. Large typography, strong contrast, editorial spacing, and oversized imagery do the heavy lifting. I designed navigation around repeated, predictable patterns (underlined links, large CTAs, carousel browsing, hover states) so the interface never gets in the way of the art. The final responsive prototype uses modular stylesheets with accessibility built in from the start.

Industry Relevance

How do you create a digital experience that honours the visual drama of art while still being functionally accessible and easy to navigate?

Cultural institutions are investing heavily in their digital presence, and the tension is always the same: how do you let the art be dramatic and immersive while keeping everything functional and accessible? This project tests a few ideas. A monochrome interface lets the artwork carry the visual weight. Minimal, predictable navigation keeps people oriented. And the homepage acts more like a digital exhibition poster, something that invites you in rather than dumping a wall of content on you.
